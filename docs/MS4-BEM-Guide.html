Welcome, Future BEM Champion!

The Brevet d'Enseignement Moyen (BEM) is an important examination at the end of middle school in Algeria. In English, you demonstrate your ability to understand written messages, use language correctly and produce meaningful writing.

This learning guide will help you understand what the examination expects and how you can prepare successfully.

📚 About this resource: The examination framework and assessment criteria presented here are drawn from the October 2018 BEM English examination construction guide. The explanations, examples, exercises and learning strategies are adapted for MS4 pupils. This is a preparation resource, not an official examination paper.
CHAPTER 01 · UNDERSTAND THE EXAM

What Does the BEM English Exam Assess?

The examination brings together three important language abilities.

20 Total examination points
02 Main examination parts
03 Assessed skill areas
06 Writing points

📖 Interpret

Understand a written text, identify its main idea, locate information and interpret words or expressions in context.

🧩 Use Language

Apply vocabulary, grammar, spelling, punctuation, word formation and pronunciation knowledge.

✍️ Produce

Write a meaningful message or text suited to a communicative situation.

🌍 Communicate

Use English to express ideas about familiar topics, personal experiences, society and the wider world.

Remember: The 2018 guide describes the MS4 exit profile in terms of interacting, interpreting and producing. The written examination assesses these competencies through reading, language activities and written production.

CHAPTER 03 · READING COMPREHENSION

Master Reading Comprehension

Discover the main types of reading activities described in the BEM guide.

📖 How Reading Comprehension Works

You read a text and complete activities to show that you understand its ideas and vocabulary.

The 2018 guide recommends a text of approximately 12–15 lines, followed by two comprehension activities and one lexis activity. The questions should reflect understanding of the text, progress from easier to more demanding tasks and follow the order in which relevant information appears.

🔎 Skimming

Read quickly to understand the general idea.

Ask yourself: What is the text mainly about?

🎯 Scanning

Look for specific information such as names, places, dates and events.

Ask yourself: Where is the answer?

🧠 Detailed Reading

Read carefully to understand facts, explanations, relationships and implied ideas.

📚 Vocabulary in Context

Use surrounding sentences to understand unfamiliar words.

01 · Multiple Choice

Select the correct answer from the proposed options.

Text: Sara joined a volunteer club to help her community.
Question: Why did Sara join the club?
a) To travel abroad
b) To help her community
c) To buy a bicycle
b) To help her community.

02 · True / False / Not Mentioned

Decide whether a statement agrees with the text, contradicts it or is not discussed.

Text: Karim visited Oran in July.
1. Karim visited Oran.
2. He travelled in December.
3. He travelled with his cousin.
1. True.
2. False — he travelled in July.
3. Not mentioned.

03 · Open Questions

Answer questions beginning with who, what, where, when, why or how.

Text: Lina planted trees to make her neighbourhood greener.
Question: Why did Lina plant trees?
To make her neighbourhood greener.

04 · Information Transfer

Complete a table, chart, diagram or fact file with information from the text.

Text: The Eiffel Tower is in Paris. It was completed in 1889.
Complete:
Location: ______
Completion year: ______
Location: Paris.
Completion year: 1889.

05 · Matching

Match headings with paragraphs, dates with events or questions with answers.

Match:
1. 1889 → a. Location
2. Paris → b. Completion year
1 → b
2 → a

06 · Main Idea / Best Title

Choose the title or sentence that best summarises the passage.

Text: A group of pupils cleaned their school, planted flowers and recycled paper.
Choose:
a) A Football Match
b) Caring for Our School
c) A Family Holiday
b) Caring for Our School.

07 · Pronoun Reference

Identify the person, place or thing represented by a pronoun.

Text: Amira is a talented pupil. She enjoys reading.
Question: What does "She" refer to?
"She" refers to Amira.

08 · Synonyms and Opposites

Find words in the passage with similar or opposite meanings.

Text: Yasmine is a generous girl. She always helps people.
Question: Find a word meaning "willing to give".
Generous.

09 · Listing Information

Identify several details, examples, similarities or differences from a text.

Text: Volunteers collected clothes, prepared meals and cleaned the park.
Question: List two activities.
Any two: collected clothes; prepared meals; cleaned the park.

10 · Inference

Use clues in the text to understand something that is not stated directly.

Text: The pupils carried umbrellas and their shoes became wet.
Question: What was the weather probably like?
It was probably raining. The umbrellas and wet shoes provide clues.
🎯 Reading strategy: Read the title and passage first. Identify the main idea, then read each question carefully. Find the relevant part of the text before answering. Do not invent information that is not supported by the passage.

📚 Grammar and Vocabulary from Your Three Sequences

These examples are revision material linked to your MS4 curriculum. They illustrate possible language skills; they are not a prediction of a particular BEM paper.

Past Simple

They (visit) London last year.
They visited London last year.

Past Passive Voice

Workers built the bridge.
Rewrite in the passive.
The bridge was built by workers.

Qualifiers

The tower is ______ tall. (extremely / yesterday)
The tower is extremely tall.

Vocabulary

Match the landmark with its type:
Eiffel Tower → tower / theatre
Tower.

Personality Traits

A person who always tells the truth is ______.
Honest.

Negative Prefixes

Form the opposite of "polite".
Impolite.

Adjective Suffixes

care + ful = ______
hope + less = ______
Careful; hopeless.

Comparisons

Samir is (tall) than his brother.
Samir is taller than his brother.

Imperative

You should protect the environment.
Express the advice using the imperative.
Protect the environment.

Conditional Type 1

If we recycle paper, we (save) trees.
If we recycle paper, we will save trees.

Gerunds after Preference Verbs

She enjoys (help) people.
She enjoys helping people.

Discourse Markers

We collect clothes. ______, we prepare meals for families in need.
(Moreover / Yesterday)
Moreover.
Important distinction: The 2018 BEM guide identifies activity categories such as transformation, editing, completion, combining and sound recognition. The specific Sequence 1–3 examples above are additional classroom practice based on your teaching programme.
CHAPTER 05 · SITUATION OF INTEGRATION

Understand Written Expression

Discover what you need to write and how your production is assessed.

✍️ What Is the Situation of Integration?

It is a communicative writing task. You are given a situation and asked to produce a meaningful written message or text.

The 2018 guide identifies three essential components of the writing instruction: CONTEXT + TASK + SUPPORT.

1️⃣ Context

Explains the situation in which you are writing.

Your English teacher asks you to introduce an important Algerian landmark to international pupils.

2️⃣ Task

Tells you what you must produce.

Write a short descriptive paragraph about the landmark.

3️⃣ Support

Provides helpful information, ideas or a fact file.

Name: Martyrs' Memorial
Location: Algiers
Purpose: Commemorates Algeria's struggle for independence

📨 Possible Formats

The guide gives examples of communicative writing formats.

Letter · Email · Dialogue · Report · Article · Social media message
OFFICIAL ASSESSMENT CRITERIA

How Are the 6 Writing Points Awarded?

The following four criteria come from the assessment grid in the 2018 guide.

2

Relevance

Are your ideas related to the topic? Did you use the required writing format?

1.5

Coherence

Are your ideas organised? Do you have a beginning, supporting sentences, a conclusion and appropriate links?

1.5

Correct Use of Language

Did you use suitable tenses, grammar, linking words and vocabulary?

1

Excellence

Did you show creativity, originality, varied vocabulary and good presentation with legible handwriting?

Writing tip: The official grid considers both the content and the required format. A well-written paragraph that does not answer the task may lose marks for relevance.

📝 Model Paragraph: Martyrs' Memorial

The colours below show the structure of a descriptive paragraph. This is a teaching model, not an official BEM answer.

TOPIC SENTENCE
Martyrs' Memorial is one of the most important landmarks in Algeria.
SUPPORTING DETAILS
It is located in Algiers, the capital city. It was inaugurated in 1982. The monument was built to commemorate the Algerians who sacrificed their lives for independence. It has three impressive concrete palm-leaf shapes. Today, many visitors come to admire this historical monument and learn about Algeria's past.
CONCLUDING SENTENCE
Martyrs' Memorial is a powerful symbol of Algeria's history and national pride.
Notice: The paragraph includes a topic sentence, supporting details and a concluding sentence. It also uses appropriate vocabulary, past passive forms and present simple description.

CHAPTER 07 · GUIDED EXAM DEMONSTRATION

Let's Explore a BEM-Style Example!

Read this original practice text, then discover how different questions work.

📖 ORIGINAL PRACTICE TEXT · SEQUENCE 3

Young Volunteers Make a Difference

Last Saturday, a group of middle school pupils organised a charity activity in their neighbourhood.

First, they collected clothes, books and school supplies for families in need. Then, they cleaned the public garden and planted small trees.

Their English teacher, Mrs Nadia, encouraged them to work together. She explained that volunteering helps young people become responsible citizens.

At the end of the day, the pupils felt proud of their achievements. They decided to organise another activity the following month.

Source: Original educational text created for this guide.

Reading tip: Identify the main idea first. Then look for specific information in each paragraph.

Activity 1 · Comprehension

Choose the correct answer:
The text is mainly about:
a) A school trip
b) A volunteer activity
c) A football competition

b) A volunteer activity.

Activity 2 · Information

Answer: What did the pupils collect?

They collected clothes, books and school supplies.

Activity 3 · Lexis

Find in the text: A word meaning "happy because you have achieved something".

Proud.

Activity 4 · Morphology

Form the opposite: responsible → ______

Irresponsible.

Activity 5 · Syntax

Complete: If pupils work together, they ______ (achieve) more.

If pupils work together, they will achieve more.

Activity 6 · Writing

Situation: Your school wants to encourage volunteering. Write a short message explaining how pupils can help their community.

Mention practical activities, explain their benefits and finish with a positive message. Use suitable linkers and correct grammar. This task requires teacher or rubric-based assessment.
This is a guided demonstration of question types, not a complete 20-point mock paper. The dedicated Reading, Language and Mock Examination destinations provide extended practice.
CHAPTER 08 · EXAM STRATEGIES

Study Smart, Answer Confidently!

Use these strategies to develop effective examination habits.

🔍 Before Answering

Read the instructions carefully.

Identify the topic and main idea.

Underline important words in questions.

Notice the marks allocated to each activity.

📖 During Reading

Read for the general meaning first.

Scan for names, dates and places.

Find evidence before choosing an answer.

Use the context to understand unfamiliar vocabulary.

🧩 During Language Tasks

Identify the required grammatical structure.

Look for tense indicators.

Check subject–verb agreement.

Read the complete sentence after answering.

✍️ During Writing

Identify the context, task and support.

Plan the main ideas.

Write connected sentences.

Check grammar, vocabulary and punctuation.

Time-management advice: Do not spend too long on one difficult question. Keep enough time to complete your written expression and review your answers. The 2018 construction guide does not prescribe a pupil-by-pupil time allocation, so any revision timetable should be treated as practice advice rather than an official rule.
