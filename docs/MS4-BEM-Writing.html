CHAPTER 01 · KNOW YOUR EXAMINATION

What Is Written Expression?

Written Expression is the second part of the BEM English examination. It assesses your ability to communicate ideas clearly and produce a meaningful, organised piece of writing.

💡

Generate Ideas

Understand the topic and select useful information before starting your paragraph.

🧱

Organise Ideas

Arrange your sentences logically and use suitable linking words.

✍️

Write Correctly

Use appropriate vocabulary, correct grammar, spelling and punctuation.

📊 Written Expression Is Worth 6 Points

According to the BEM framework supplied for this project, Written Expression represents 6 points out of 20. Your paragraph is assessed through relevance, coherence, correct use of language and excellence.

6/20
Remember: The practice missions in this academy use an 8–10 sentence target to help you develop a complete paragraph. Always follow the exact instructions of the examination task.
CHAPTER 02 · BEM ASSESSMENT

Understand the 6-Point Marking Grid

Knowing how writing is assessed helps you focus on the qualities of a successful paragraph.

🎯

Relevance

2 POINTS

Respond to the task, respect the topic and include the required information.

🔗

Coherence

1.5 POINTS

Organise ideas logically and connect sentences using appropriate linking words.

📝

Correct Use of Language

1.5 POINTS

Use appropriate grammar, vocabulary, spelling and punctuation.

🌟

Excellence

1 POINT

Produce effective, thoughtful writing with suitable expressions and careful presentation.

TOTAL = 2 + 1.5 + 1.5 + 1 = 6 POINTS
Important: The interactive self-assessment later in this lesson is a reflection tool. It cannot replace a teacher's evaluation of your actual writing.
CHAPTER 03 · BEFORE YOU WRITE

Understand the Writing Task

Before writing, identify the context, the task and the support.

🌍 01 · Context

The context introduces the situation and explains why you are writing.

Your school is organising an exhibition about famous landmarks.

🎯 02 · Task

The task tells you exactly what you must produce. Look for the instruction verb and the expected text.

Write a short descriptive paragraph about a famous landmark.

📋 03 · Support

The support provides information or ideas that help you complete the task.

Name: Big Ben
Location: London
Completion: 1859
Height: about 96 metres
Writing strategy: Underline the instruction verb, circle the topic and identify every detail you need to include.
CHAPTER 04 · BUILD A PARAGRAPH

The Three Parts of a Good Paragraph

A successful paragraph has a clear beginning, a developed middle and an effective ending.

01

Introduction

Introduce the main topic. Tell the reader what or who you are describing.

Big Ben is one of the most famous landmarks in London.
02

Supporting Details

Develop your paragraph with useful facts, descriptions and examples.

It is part of the Palace of Westminster. The tower is about 96 metres tall.
03

Conclusion

End your paragraph with a final thought about the topic.

Today, it remains an important symbol of London.
Useful linking words: First · Next · Then · After that · Finally · Moreover · In addition · Also · However · Therefore Choose linking words according to the relationship between your ideas. Do not use them randomly.
CHAPTER 05 · LEARN FROM A MODEL

Explore a Model Paragraph

Notice how the introduction, supporting details and conclusion work together.

🕰️ BIG BEN · LONDON

🏛️ A Famous London Landmark

INTRODUCTION
Big Ben is one of the most famous landmarks in London, England.
SUPPORTING DETAILS
It is part of the Palace of Westminster. The famous clock tower is officially called Elizabeth Tower. It was completed in 1859. It is about 96 metres tall. The tower has a beautiful clock on each of its four sides. It attracts many visitors every year. People around the world recognise its impressive appearance.
CONCLUSION
Today, Big Ben remains an important symbol of London and British history.
Observe: The paragraph introduces the landmark, provides facts and descriptions, and ends with a concluding sentence.
