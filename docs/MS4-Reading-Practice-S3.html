MISSION 01 · GOOD CITIZENSHIP

A Young Citizen Who Made a Difference

Read the passage and discover how responsible actions can improve a community.

📖 Read the Text

In a peaceful neighbourhood in Sétif, Algeria, lived a fourteen-year-old pupil named Karim. He was a responsible, helpful and environmentally conscious boy. Every morning, he walked to school and noticed rubbish scattered along the streets. Although the neighbourhood was beautiful, some residents did not respect public spaces.

One day, Karim decided to take action. He believed that everyone had a responsibility to protect the environment. First, he discussed the problem with his English teacher. Then, he invited his classmates to organise a neighbourhood cleaning campaign. Many pupils immediately agreed to participate.

On Saturday morning, the young volunteers gathered near their school. They wore gloves and collected plastic bottles, paper and other waste. Some pupils planted small trees, while others prepared colourful posters encouraging residents to keep their streets clean.

At first, only a few adults joined the campaign. However, when neighbours saw the pupils' enthusiasm, more people offered to help. By the end of the day, the streets looked much cleaner and more attractive. The local community thanked the volunteers for their excellent work.

Karim was extremely proud of his classmates. He realised that being a good citizen does not simply mean obeying laws. It also means respecting others, protecting public property and contributing positively to society. His small initiative proved that young people can make a real difference.

🌱 Reading Strategy

Identify the problem:
What problem did the characters notice?
Identify the solution:
What actions did they take?
Identify the result:
How did their actions improve the community?
MISSION 02 · VOLUNTEERING & CHARITY

Small Actions, Big Changes

Read the passage and discover the importance of solidarity and voluntary work.

📖 Read the Text

Volunteering is one of the most meaningful ways to help others and improve society. Every year, millions of people around the world offer their time, energy and skills without expecting money in return. They work with charity organisations, support vulnerable people and participate in community projects.

In Algeria, many young volunteers take part in charitable activities. Some collect clothes and school supplies for disadvantaged children. Others distribute food baskets to families in need, especially during the holy month of Ramadan. These activities promote solidarity, generosity and cooperation.

Last winter, a group of secondary school pupils organised a charity campaign called "Warm Hearts". Their objective was to help families living in a remote mountain village. First, they collected warm clothes, blankets and food. Next, they sorted the donations and packed them into boxes. Finally, they worked with a local charity association to deliver the supplies to the families.

The campaign was extremely successful. Many families received essential supplies, and the volunteers felt proud of their achievements. However, the pupils also discovered that charity work requires organisation, patience and teamwork. They learnt that even simple actions can bring happiness to people who face difficult circumstances.

Volunteering benefits both individuals and communities. It helps people develop communication skills, confidence and a sense of responsibility. Moreover, it strengthens relationships and encourages citizens to support one another. As the young volunteers discovered, we do not need to be wealthy to make someone's life better. Sometimes, kindness and a little time are the greatest gifts we can offer.

🤝 Reading Strategy

Scanning:
Look for the name of the campaign, the beneficiaries and the donated items.
Sequencers:
Pay attention to first, next and finally.
Inference:
Think about the values and lessons communicated by the text.
MISSION 03 · GLOBALISATION

Globalisation: A Connected World

Complete this challenging BEM-style reading comprehension mission.

📖 Read the Text

Today, our world is more connected than ever before. People from different countries can communicate, exchange information, buy products and discover new cultures more easily. This growing connection between countries is known as globalisation. It influences education, technology, business, entertainment and many aspects of everyday life.

One of the greatest advantages of globalisation is the rapid exchange of knowledge. Thanks to the internet, students can attend online lessons, explore digital libraries and communicate with learners from different parts of the world. For example, an Algerian pupil can practise English with a student living in Canada or watch an educational video produced in Britain.

Globalisation also encourages international trade. Products manufactured in one country can be sold in many others. Consequently, consumers often have access to a wider variety of goods. Furthermore, cultural exchanges allow people to discover different traditions, languages, music and food. These experiences can promote understanding and tolerance.

However, globalisation also presents challenges. Small local businesses sometimes struggle to compete with large international companies. In addition, the widespread use of global media may influence local traditions and cultural identities. Another serious problem is environmental pollution caused by some forms of production and long-distance transportation.

Therefore, globalisation should be approached responsibly. Governments, companies and citizens must cooperate to protect the environment, respect cultural diversity and promote fair opportunities. Young people should benefit from global connections while preserving their national identity. If we use technology wisely and respect other cultures, we can help build a more peaceful and sustainable world.

🌍 BEM Strategy

Advantages:
Identify the positive effects of globalisation.
Disadvantages:
Find the challenges and negative effects.
Discourse markers:
Notice however, moreover, furthermore, consequently and therefore.
Inference:
Understand the writer's message and suggested solutions.
