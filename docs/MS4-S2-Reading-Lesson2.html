The Ideal Teacher

Adam's personal story
📜
01

My ideal teacher is Mr Karim, my English teacher at middle school. I first met him when I was twelve. At that time, I was a quiet pupil who was often afraid of making mistakes. I liked English, but I was not very confident when I had to speak in front of the class. Mr Karim was different from some teachers I had known before. He was calm, patient and friendly, and he always listened carefully to his pupils. From the first lesson, I felt that he wanted everyone to learn, not only the strongest pupils.

02

In his classroom, everyone has a chance to participate. Mr Karim explains difficult ideas clearly and gives us enough time to think. When a pupil gives a wrong answer, he does not laugh or become angry. Instead, he says that mistakes are part of learning and encourages the pupil to try again. I remember one lesson when I was unable to complete an exercise. While I was looking at my notebook without knowing what to do, Mr Karim came to my desk and explained the first step. Suddenly, the exercise seemed less difficult.

03

Mr Karim also believes that every pupil has a strength. Some pupils are good at speaking, while others are excellent at writing, drawing or organising ideas. He often gives us different tasks so that we can discover what we are good at. Unlike a teacher who only gives instructions, he works with us and listens to our ideas. He is helpful and respectful, but he is also demanding. He expects us to be hardworking and responsible because he knows that success requires effort. His lessons are sometimes challenging, but they are never boring.

04

One of my favourite memories was a class project about people who had made a difference in our community. Mr Karim divided us into small groups and asked us to research, discuss and present our work. At first, I was nervous because I had never spoken for several minutes in front of an audience. My classmates encouraged me, and Mr Karim gave me useful advice before my presentation. I prepared carefully, practised several times and finally stood in front of the class. My voice was not perfect, but I continued. When I finished, everyone applauded. I felt proud because I realised that I could do something I had once thought impossible.

05

Today, I understand that a good teacher does much more than explain lessons. An ideal teacher helps pupils become more confident, independent and responsible. Mr Karim has taught me that being unsuccessful at first does not mean that I will always fail. He has also shown me that a careless mistake can become a useful lesson if I learn from it. I do not know exactly what I will become in the future, but I know that I will need determination and hard work to achieve my goals. Mr Karim will probably never know how important his encouragement has been to me, but I will always remember him as one of the most inspiring people in my school life.
