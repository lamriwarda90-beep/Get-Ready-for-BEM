Zohra Drif: A Life of Courage and Memory

01

Zohra Drif was born on 28 December 1934 in the Tiaret region of French Algeria, on the Sersou plain. Her family valued education, and her father, Ahmed Drif, was a qadi, or judge, and a scholar. She grew up in Vialar, now called Tissemsilt. In her memoir, she remembers how her mother taught her about Algerian history, customs, language, religion and the stories of her ancestors. These childhood memories helped shape the young girl's sense of identity.

02

Zohra was also a very good student. She attended a French colonial school and remembers being one of the few Algerian girls in her classes. At primary school, she was especially successful and even tied for first place with her friend Roselyne Garcia. When Roselyne later failed an entrance exam, Zohra comforted her. The experience remained in her memory because it made her think about the opportunities available to Algerian girls. In 1947, she entered Lycée Fromentin in Algiers, where she continued her studies.

03

In 1954, Zohra began studying law at the University of Algiers. The same year, the Algerian War of Independence began. Gradually, she became involved in the Algerian nationalist movement and in clandestine activities in Algiers. During the Battle of Algiers, she took part in the urban resistance. On 30 September 1956, she participated in the bombing at the Milk Bar in Algiers. The attack killed three young women and injured other people. This was one of the events that later became associated with the Battle of Algiers.

04

Life as a freedom fighter was very different from her childhood. She had to live secretly and make difficult decisions in a dangerous situation. Her memoir does not present the period as a simple story of heroes and enemies. Instead, she looks back on the war and describes fear, loss, difficult choices and the responsibilities that came with her actions. Her experiences changed her understanding of courage, loyalty and the price of freedom.

05

On 22 September 1957, Zohra Drif was arrested in the Casbah of Algiers. She was later tried by a military tribunal and, in August 1958, received a sentence of 20 years of hard labour. She spent about five years in prison, in Algeria and later in France. During this period, she continued her studies and wrote about her experiences. She was released in 1962 after Algeria gained independence. Her childhood memories, her years as a student and her experiences during the war all became important parts of the story she later told about her life.

06

After independence, Zohra Drif became a lawyer and a public figure. She was elected to Algeria's first National Constituent Assembly in 1962 and later served in the Council of the Nation. Many years later, she published her memoir, Inside the Battle of Algiers: Memoir of a Woman Freedom Fighter . Looking back at her childhood and wartime experiences, she shows how a person's life can contain very different chapters. Her story connects education, identity, friendship, difficult experiences and determination. It also invites readers to think carefully about history and about the human experiences behind important historical events.

1 · Present Simple

2 · Past Simple

4 · Future with Will

“Her story will remain important to readers.”

5 · Superlative

“It became one of the most important experiences of her life.”

6 · Contrast

“Unlike her peaceful childhood, her wartime life was dangerous.”
